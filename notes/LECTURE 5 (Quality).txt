# Quality and Productivity Management

--- slide 3 ---
# Introduction
  In engineering, the concepts of quality and productivity are inseparable from the goals of effective design, manufacturing, and service delivery. 
  In today’s competitive global economy, customers expect not only excellent product performance but also timely delivery and reasonable pricing. 
  To meet these expectations, organizations must focus on both producing high-quality outputs and doing so efficiently—this is the domain of quality and productivity management.
  Quality ensures that the product or service is fit for use, while productivity ensures that it is delivered efficiently, using the least possible resources without compromising standards. A systematic approach to managing both leads to organizational excellence and long-term sustainability.

# Quality in Engineering
  Quality can be defined as the ability of a product, process, or service to consistently meet or exceed customer requirements and expectations. In engineering, quality is multifaceted and includes aspects such as durability, performance, reliability, safety, and aesthetic value.
  
  Dimensions of Product Quality (as identified by David A. Garvin): 
  1. Performance – Primary operating characteristics. 
  2. Features – Supplementary elements that enhance appeal. 
  3. Reliability – Probability of failure-free performance. 
  4. Conformance – Degree to which product design meets specifications. 
  5. Durability – Product life span before replacement. 
  6. Serviceability – Ease and cost of repair or servicing. 
  7. Aesthetics – Sensory characteristics. 
  8. Perceived Quality – Image and brand influence.

--- slide 12 ---
  1. Performance
  Definition: The primary operating characteristics of a product.
  Example: A car’s acceleration, fuel efficiency, or top speed.
  2. Features
  Definition: Additional characteristics or secondary functions that enhance appeal or usability.
  Example: Bluetooth, GPS, or heated seats in a car.
  3. Reliability
  Definition: The likelihood that a product will function without failure for a given period.
  Example: A computer that runs smoothly without crashing for years.
  4. Conformance
  Definition: The degree to which the product meets design and operating specifications.
  Example: Manufactured gears having exact tolerances as per the design.

--- slide 13 ---
  5. Durability
  Definition: The amount of use a product can sustain before it deteriorates or fails.
  Example: The lifespan of a smartphone battery or an aircraft engine.
  6. Serviceability
  Definition: How easy it is to maintain, repair, or upgrade the product.
  Example: A modular laptop where parts can be easily replaced.
  7. Aesthetics
  Definition: The look, feel, sound, taste, or smell of the product — subjective to users.
  Example: The sleek design and feel of a premium smartphone.
  8. Perceived Quality
  Definition: The customer’s perception of quality based on brand image, reputation, or prior experience.
  Example: Apple products are often seen as high-quality due to branding and past performance.

# SIX SIGMA APPROACH
  Initiated by Motorola in 1987, Six Sigma is a business strategy for process improvement and corporate growth (Antony 2004) that provides a disciplined and structured approach of using both statistical and non-statistical tools and techniques within its improvement and design methodologies.  
  
  The Six Sigma approach is a data-driven methodology aimed at improving business processes by eliminating defects and minimizing variability. It is widely used in manufacturing, service industries, healthcare, and other sectors to enhance quality and efficiency.

--- slide 15 ---
# Key Objectives of Six Sigma
  Reduce defects (errors or inconsistencies)
  Improve process capability and performance
  Enhance customer satisfaction
  Lower operational costs

--- slide 16 ---

  Principle
  Description

  Process focus

  Proactive management
  Preventing issues instead of reacting to them.
  Collaboration
  Teamwork and cross-functional engagement.
  Continuous improvement
  Ongoing effort to improve performance.

--- slide 17 ---
# DMAIC Methodology 
  (for improving existing processes)
  Step
  Description
  D – Define
  Identify the problem, goals, and customer requirements.
  M – Measure
  Collect data to understand current performance.
  A – Analyse
  Identify root causes of defects or variations.
  I – Improve
  Implement and test solutions to eliminate root causes.
  C – Control
  Monitor the improved process to sustain gains.

--- slide 18 ---
# DMADV Methodology 
  (for creating new processes/products)
  Step
  Description
  D – Define
  Project goals and customer needs.
  M – Measure
  Determine customer needs and specifications.
  A – Analyze
  Design alternatives and evaluate options.
  D – Design
  Develop detailed design and verify.
  V – Verify
  Validate design through pilot testing and feedback.
