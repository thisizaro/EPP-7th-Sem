# Production and Productivity
# Production is concerned with the activity of producing goods and/or services.
  In quantitative terms, it is the quantity of output produced.
  Productivity is concerned with the efficient utilization of resources (inputs) producing goods and/or services (output).
  In quantitative terms, it is the ratio of the output produced to input(s) used.
  Productivity = output produced/input used

--- Slide 3 ---
# Example: 1
  A company manufacturing electronic calculators produced 10,000 calculators employing 50 persons working at 8 hours/day for 25 days. What are the values of production and productivity during this period?
  Production = 10,000 calculators
  Productivity =  calculator/person-hour
  Check that suppose the company used 10 additional persons and produced 20 % more calculators, production went up, but not productivity.

# Efficiency and Effectiveness
# Efficiency is the ratio of actual output achieved to standard output expected.
  Efficiency= actual output achieved / standard output expected
  That is, efficiency is concerned with how well the inputs are utilized.
  Effectiveness is the degree of accomplishment of objectives.
  That is, effectiveness is concerned with how well the outputs accomplish their
  intended goals.

--- Slide 6 ---
# Partial, Total-Factor, and Total Productivity
# Partial productivity is the ratio of output to one class of inputs.
  Examples: Labour productivity, material productivity, Capital productivity
  Total-Factor Productivity is the ratio of net output to the sum of the associated labour and capital inputs.
  Net output = Total output – Intermediate goods and services purchased
  Total Productivity is the ratio of total output to the sum of all inputs.
  Total Productivity=Total output/sum of all inputs

# Service Productivity
# It is seldom possible to define “one unit of a service.”
  Productivity measurements in services are normally only partial measurements:
  - How many customers are served by a restaurant waiter in a day
  - How many phone calls are dispatched by a call center employee.
  Reducing input improves input efficiency, but can reduce quality, hence output, and hence productivity.
  Banks offer ATM services and insurance companies offer call centre services.  QoS has not deteriorated.  Hence these are effective schemes.
