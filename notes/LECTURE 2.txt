# Who is a Practicing Engineer
  A practicing engineer is someone who applies engineering principles to solve real-world problems and is actively involved in the engineering profession
  A practicing engineer can be a Professional Engineer (PE) who is licensed to offer engineering services to the public or someone who is gaining experience under the supervision of a PE.

--- slide 5 ---
# Key Attributes of a Practicing Engineer
  Technical Proficiency – Mastery in core engineering principles
  Ethical Integrity – Adheres to codes of ethics and professional conduct
  Problem Solving Skills – Analytical and innovative mindset
  Communication Skills – Effective oral and written communication
  Teamwork and Collaboration – Works well in multidisciplinary teams
  Adaptability and Lifelong Learning – Stays updated with technological advancements

--- slide 6 ---
# Additional Attributes
  Project Management Skills – Planning, executing, and leading projects
  Decision Making under Constraints – Balancing cost, time, quality, and safety
  Social and Environmental Responsibility – Understanding sustainability and societal impact
  Leadership Qualities – Influencing, guiding, and mentoring others

--- slide 7 ---
# Core Functions of a Practicing Engineer
  Design and Development – Creating products, processes, or systems
  Analysis and Testing – Evaluating performance and ensuring quality
  Implementation and Operation – Overseeing production and daily functions
  Maintenance and Support – Ensuring long-term functionality

# Engineer as a designer
  The engineering design process is a series of steps that engineers follow to find a solution to a problem. The steps include problem solving processes such as, for example, determining your objectives and constraints, prototyping, testing and evaluation.

--- slide 10 ---
# Engineer as a change agent
  A change agent is someone who:
  Identifies areas for improvement
  Initiates innovation
  Implements new solutions
  Influences others toward positive transformation

--- slide 11 ---
  Engineer as a Change Agent: Key Aspects
  
  1. Technological Transformation
  Engineers introduce new technologies (e.g., AI, renewable energy, electric vehicles).
  Example: Electrical engineers helped shift from fossil fuels to solar and wind power, transforming the energy sector.
  2. Solving Societal Challenges
  Engineers design systems to solve global issues like clean water, climate change, healthcare access.
  Example: Engineers Without Borders (EWB) works to design low-cost water purification systems in rural areas.
  3. Sustainable Development
  Engineers promote eco-friendly designs and sustainable practices.
  Example: Green buildings, biodegradable materials, and energy-efficient transport systems.

--- slide 12 ---
  4. Economic Growth
  By developing efficient products and systems, engineers boost productivity and economic development.
  Example: Automation in manufacturing has revolutionized global supply chains.
  5. Digital Innovation
  Engineers are central to the digital revolution, creating smart systems and AI-based solutions.
  Example: Software engineers developing health apps that monitor and predict disease.
  6. Disaster Management & Recovery
  Engineers help design systems for early warning, rescue operations, and rebuilding.
  Example: Structural engineers redesign buildings to be earthquake-resistant.
